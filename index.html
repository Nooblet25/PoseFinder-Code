Art tips

A few habits worth building into every reference session, whether you're doing a 30-second gesture or a full study.

Gesture first

Draw the movement before the outline

Find the line of action running through the spine before you touch a single contour. The gesture is what makes a pose feel alive — details can't rescue a stiff gesture.

Timing

Practice at more than one speed

30-second poses build instinct; 10-minute poses build accuracy. Rotate between both instead of settling into one comfortable pace.

Negative space

Check the shapes around the figure, not just on it

The space between an arm and the torso is often easier to get right than the arm itself — and it will tell you immediately if a proportion is off.

Values

Block in light and shadow before detail

Squint at the reference and place two or three value shapes first. Detail added on top of correct values reads as form; detail added without it just reads as texture.

Foreshortening

Draw the near shape big and simple

Whatever's closest to the viewer should dominate the silhouette. Resist the urge to shrink it just because it "looks too big" on the page.

Review

Flip your sketch to check it cold

Mirroring a drawing (or just looking at it upside down) breaks your familiarity with it and makes proportion errors jump out immediately.

Meet the PoseFinder team

PoseFinder: A Web-Based 3D Pose-Based Human Reference Image Retrieval System is an undergraduate thesis project developed by four Bachelor of Science in Computer Science students specializing in Graphics and Visualization at Laguna State Polytechnic University – San Pablo City Campus. The team collaboratively works on system development, research, documentation, testing, and user interface refinement to develop an accessible and functional pose-reference tool.

Iris Murciela F. Yenko
Lead Developer

Oversees the technical development of PoseFinder, including the implementation of its core features, 3D pose interaction, API integration, and system functionality. Also works on optimizing and refining the system based on testing results and research requirements.
